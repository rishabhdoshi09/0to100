# Parents and learning mode

QuantTerm is written so that even a curious 12-year-old can understand **what it does,
what it does not do, and why it refuses to trade** sometimes. That is a **clarity
benchmark** — it is **not** permission for a child to place real-money trades.

## The firm rule
Any learning experience for children or beginners must stay **completely separate from
real-money trading.**

- The **Practice Walkthrough** uses **made-up** stocks ("Practice Ltd") and touches no
  broker, no Telegram order, and no live market service.
- **Paper mode** uses pretend money only.
- **Live (real-money) mode is locked** and is not part of any learning flow.

## Good ways to use QuantTerm to learn
- Do the **Practice Walkthrough** and talk about each step: why one setup qualified,
  why another was skipped, what a stop is, what the most you could lose is.
- Read the **plain "why"** on skipped setups — it teaches patience and rules.
- Discuss **"no trade can be the right answer"** and why chasing is dangerous.
- Look at **PASS / FAIL / INCONCLUSIVE** and talk about honest evidence.

## What to teach (the important lessons)
- A good day is **following the rules**, not making money.
- Past results do **not** guarantee the future.
- Never risk money you cannot afford to lose.
- Missing information is **unknown**, not zero.

## What NOT to do
- Do not let a child (or a beginner) switch anything towards LIVE.
- Do not treat practice profit as real profit.
- Do not use real money as a "reward" for a good practice run.

There are no real-money rewards, no celebratory gambling language, and no profit
guarantees anywhere in QuantTerm — on purpose.
